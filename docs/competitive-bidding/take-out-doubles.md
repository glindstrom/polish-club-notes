# Take-Out Doubles

### Description

- **Context**: Opponents open at the one level.
- **Meaning**: At least 3 cards in each unbid major (occasionally 2 in one major).
- **Strength**: 12+ points in direct position (if not a passed hand); 9+ points in protective position or as a passed hand.

    | -------- | ---- | ------ | ---- | --- |
    |          | 1♣   | Double | Pass | ?   |

    | Response    | Meaning                             |
    | ----------- | ----------------------------------- |
    | Pass        | Lots of clubs                       |
    | 1♦          | 0–6 points                          |
    | 1♥/1♠       | 7+ points, usually 4 cards, forcing |
    | 2♣          | Artificial, forcing               |

    | 3♣/3♦/3♥/3♠ | 9–11 points, 5-card suit            |
    | 4♥/4♠       | 6+ card suit                        |

    | Doubler’s Bid | Meaning                                                        |
    | ------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
    | 1♥/1♠         | At least 3-card suit                                           |
    | 1NT           | 18–20 points                                                   |
    | 2♣            | 15–17 points, 5+ clubs, denies 4 cards in a major, non-forcing |
    | 2♦            | Game force, denies two-suiters                                 |

    | 3♠            | 5-5 in spades and diamonds, game force                         |

??? note "Bidding after Opponent’s Redouble"
    ### Bidding after Opponent’s Redouble

    | Sequence | They | We     | They     | We  |
    | -------- | ---- | ------ | -------- | --- |
    |          | 1♥   | Double | Redouble | ?   |

    | Response    | Meaning                                      |
    | ----------- | -------------------------------------------- |
    | Pass        | No suitable suit to bid                      |
    | 1♠/2♣/2♦    | No strength; the suit you prefer to play     |
    | 2NT         | At least 5-5 in the minors                   |
    | 2♠          | Preemptive                                   |
    | 3♣/3♦       | Preemptive                                   |
    | Suit jump   | Preemptive                                   |

    Fourth hand’s redouble marks this hand as weak: the opponents and partner have shown at least 33 HCP. Do not double their two-level escape without trump length. The doubler normally passes a preemptive jump. After 1♥ (X) XX, bid 1♠ with ♠J964 ♥J1092 ♦J65 ♣63, and 2NT with ♠6 ♥104 ♦J10764 ♣109875. After 1♦ (X) XX, 2♠ on ♠J10752 ♥98 ♦J86 ♣1092 is preemptive and the doubler passes. Izdebski, School 1–3/2015; 1–3/2022. See [Penalty Doubles, Redoubles and Passes](penalty-doubles-and-passes.md).

    #### After they respond 1NT and it is passed out
    After (1♥) Pass (1NT) Pass (Pass), or (1♠) in the same auction, double is a trap pass and therefore penalty. Izdebski, School 10–12/2014.

??? note "Double of a Three- or Four-Level Preempt"
    ### Double of a Three- or Four-Level Preempt

    The double is take-out. 4NT over their four of a major is a 5-5 two-suiter, not Blackwood.

    Partner passes to convert in two cases: a worthless hand, or a balanced defensive hand with no safe road to game. With unbalanced offensive shape, partner bids. When they have already shown 9+ trumps, for example (2♠) Pass (3♠) Double, the double is take-out with shortage: bid your own suit.

    Pass is this conversion. The positive bids after a double of their 2♥/2♠ stay as in [Defence Against Specific Openings](defence-specific-openings.md). Examples: [Penalty Doubles, Redoubles and Passes](penalty-doubles-and-passes.md). Izdebski, School 4–6/2015; 7–9/2019.
