# Penalty Doubles, Redoubles and Passes

Judgement from Władysław Izdebski’s School articles in *Świat Brydża* on penalty doubles, redoubles and competitive passes. A bid that another page already defines keeps that meaning; those cases are stated under the principle. The Cuebids statistics that accompany the articles are not part of these notes.

These redoubles are responder’s redouble of a take-out double. The [support redouble](../after-intervention/support-double-redouble.md) is a different agreement.

## What the redouble promises

After our one-level opening and their take-out double, responder’s redouble shows at least 10 HCP. It invites opener to penalty-double if they escape at the one- or two-level. The redouble shows strength without naming a suit.

That 10+ redouble is already our agreement after [1♣](../openings/one-club-interference.md), [1♦](../openings/one-diamond-interference.md) and [1♥/1♠](../openings/one-heart-one-spade-interference.md). Two limits on those pages stay:

- After 1♣ the redouble still promises at least three clubs, and opener may pass 1♣ redoubled.
- After 1♥/1♠ the redouble is a balanced 10+ hand. It does not ask to play the redoubled contract. With values in their suit, pass.

After a negative double (Sputnik) of our one-level overcall, redouble shows 10+ HCP and a doubleton in partner’s suit. With three or more cards in partner’s suit, raise with the support bid we already play. Where that raise is limited, a stronger hand cue-bids their suit: after a 1♦ overcall, 2♦ stays 6–10, and a stronger diamond fit cue-bids. The case (1♦) 1♠ (X) is defined on [Overcalls at the One Level](overcalls-one-level.md).

Example: (1♦) 1♠ (X) with ♠K6 ♥A95 ♦A872 ♣10843. The bid is redouble. With a real spade fit, Izdebski cue-bids 2♦. Our support bids after this double already show the fit, so the cue-bid is not added.

Izdebski, School 7–9/2022; 10–12/2019.

## When partner then bids

Once responder has redoubled, opener’s free bid in his own suit is a minimum and not forcing. It applies after a limited opening (1♦, 1♥, 1♠). Opener’s rebids after 1♣ are unchanged, because 1♣ can be 18+.

After 1♥ (X) XX (1♠), 2♥ shows a minimum opening with a good six-card suit that does not want to penalize spades, typical ♠x ♥KQJxxx ♦Qxx ♣QJx. The redoubler passes. The same call after 1♠ is 2♠. Do not pull partner’s penalty double of their escape.

Example: ♠QJ54 ♥32 ♦KJ98 ♣K98 after 1♥ (X) XX (1♠) 2♥. The bid is pass.

Izdebski, School 7–9/2022.

## Do not always redouble on strength

Choose a bid that shows the suit or the fit when the redouble would only show points. Three cases:

- After 1♦ (X), a hand with a good suit and a diamond fit wants to bid the suit. Izdebski bids 1♥ with ♠53 ♥KQ983 ♦K1085 ♣Q10. Our 1♥ in that sequence is 5–9 with good suit quality, and this hand is about 11 HCP, so it starts with redouble. The redouble [does not deny diamond support](../openings/one-diamond-interference.md). Show the diamonds later.
- Opposite a third-seat opening that may be light, pass with a minimum balanced hand and show the strength on the next round. A jump after an immediate redouble is read as preemptive. Izdebski passes ♠AJ4 ♥32 ♦AJ876 ♣982 after P (P) 1♥ (X). That hand has five diamonds, so in our system it bids 2♣, the forcing transfer to diamonds.
- When our three-level preempt is doubled, pass and double later if they escape. Example: 3♦ (X) with ♠QJ76 ♥KJ32 ♦6 ♣A863. The bid is pass. After a [3NT opening](../openings/three-level-openings.md), redouble still shows stoppers in the side suits. After 2♥/2♠ doubled, bid as if there had been no double.

Izdebski, School 10–12/2019; 1–3/2022.

## Doubler’s partner after a redouble

When fourth hand redoubles, the doubler's partner is typically weak: the opponents and partner have shown at least 33 HCP. A simple suit bid promises no strength. It names the suit you prefer to play. Every jump is preemptive. 2NT, which cannot be a strong bid, shows at least 5-5 in the minors. Do not double their two-level escape without trump length, and normally pass partner’s preemptive jump.

The bids are tabulated under [Bidding after Opponent’s Redouble](take-out-doubles.md).

Example: after 1♥ (X) XX, bid 1♠ with ♠J964 ♥J1092 ♦J65 ♣63, and 2NT with ♠6 ♥104 ♦J10764 ♣109875. After 1♦ (X) XX, 2♠ is preemptive, typical ♠J10752 ♥98 ♦J86 ♣1092, and the doubler passes.

A redouble of our *reopening* double is a different seat. Pass there is still “I want to play it redoubled”.

Izdebski, School 1–3/2015; 1–3/2022.

## Take-out double of a preempt

A double of a three- or four-level preempt is take-out. 4NT over their four of a major shows a 5-5 two-suiter, not Blackwood. Blackwood is unchanged once a suit is agreed.

Partner converts to penalty by passing in two cases: a worthless hand, or a reasonably strong balanced defensive hand with no safe road to game. With unbalanced offensive shape, bid.

Example: after (4♠) X (P), pass with the worthless ♠J73 ♥1064 ♦J8765 ♣104, and pass with the balanced ♠75 ♥K1064 ♦KQ95 ♣Q94. With ♠3 ♥KQ10874 ♦KJ95 ♣74 bid at the five-level. With ♠3 ♥QJ974 ♦QJ987 ♣42, 4NT is take-out in the red suits.

When they have already raised each other to at least nine trumps, the double is take-out with shortage in their suit. Bid your own suit. The sequence is (2♠) P (3♠) X.

Pass is the conversion. The positive bids in our [Lebensohl defence to 2♥/2♠](defence-specific-openings.md) stay as written.

Izdebski, School 4–6/2015; 7–9/2019.

## Sitting for partner’s double

When you have already shown a balanced hand and partner penalty-doubles, or reopens with a double, pass unless the hand has undisclosed extreme shape: a void together with 5-5 or with a six-card suit, or a suit of seven or more.

After 1♥ (1NT) X (P), or 1♠ (1NT) X (P), opener normally passes. The double is for penalty. After 1♣ and 1♦ the same double is 8+ points, and 1♥/1♠ use that threshold. Izdebski describes the double as about 9+ HCP. Pull at unfavorable vulnerability, with a side singleton, or with a very unbalanced hand.

Example: after 1♥ (1NT) X (P), pass with ♠J10 ♥AQ1074 ♦KQ8 ♣J92. Bid 2♦ with ♠J9 ♥K10654 ♦QJ876 ♣A.

After (1NT) X (P), 8+ HCP and a balanced hand converts to penalty. A weak or unbalanced hand removes to its own contract. Our [defence to a weak 1NT](defence-specific-openings.md) already passes with 8+ balanced. The removal bids on that page stay: a natural 0–7 suit, or an 8+ transfer. We do not play 2♣ as pass-or-correct there. Our double of a strong 1NT is Multi-Landy, a five-card minor and a four-card major, so this conversion does not apply to it.

After a major opening, (1♥) P (1NT) P (P) X, and the same with 1♠, is a trap pass and therefore penalty. See [Take-Out Doubles](take-out-doubles.md).

A reopening pass is not forcing.

Izdebski, School 10–12/2014; 7–9/2015; 1–3/2018; 4–6/2018; 1–3/2015; 7–9/2019.

## Passes at the four- and five-level

A pass over their competitive bid at the four- or five-level is normally not forcing, especially when our own game bid was competitive. Once partner has doubled, for example (5♣) P (P) X, do not pull without a clear offensive reason. The gap between their game and our penalty can be small, and a double can make the play easier. The conclusion depends on the type of auction.

Two agreements already on the card stay, and they match this where the pass is non-forcing:

- After a take-out double and their jump to four of a suit, pass is non-forcing. See [Take-Out Doubles](take-out-doubles.md) and [General Principles](general-principles.md).
- After (3♣/3♦) double (5♣/5♦), pass is non-forcing except at unfavorable vulnerability, where pass is forcing and double discourages bidding.

Izdebski, School 1–3/2015; 7–9/2019.
