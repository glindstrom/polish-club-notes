# Penalty Doubles, Redoubles and Passes

Judgement from Władysław Izdebski’s School articles in *Świat Brydża* on penalty doubles, redoubles and competitive passes. These are the agreements. The sequence pages state the same bids. The Cuebids statistics that accompany the articles are not part of these notes.

These redoubles are responder’s redouble of a take-out double. The [support redouble](../after-intervention/support-double-redouble.md) is a different agreement.

## What the redouble promises

After our one-level opening and their take-out double, responder’s redouble shows at least 10 HCP and no shape. It invites opener to penalty-double if they escape at the one- or two-level. The redouble shows strength without naming a suit or a fit.

That is the redouble after [1♣](../openings/one-club-interference.md), [1♦](../openings/one-diamond-interference.md) and [1♥/1♠](../openings/one-heart-one-spade-interference.md).

- After 1♣ the redouble does not promise club length. Opener does not pass 1♣ redoubled.
- After 1♥/1♠ the redouble is 10+ HCP and does not ask to play the redoubled contract. With values in their suit, pass.

After a negative double (Sputnik) of our one-level overcall, redouble shows 10+ HCP and a doubleton in partner’s suit. With three or more cards in partner’s suit, raise. Do not redouble with a fit. Transfers apply after the double. A 6–10 hand with 3+ cards in partner’s suit still raises. See [Overcalls at the One Level](overcalls-one-level.md).

Example: (1♦) 1♠ (X) with ♠K6 ♥A95 ♦A872 ♣10843. The bid is redouble.

Izdebski, School 7–9/2022; 10–12/2019.

## When partner then bids

Once responder has redoubled, opener’s free bid in his own suit is a minimum and not forcing. This includes 1♣. A strong 1♣ hand does not make that call. The redoubler passes.

After 1♥ (X) XX (1♠), 2♥ shows a minimum opening with a good six-card suit that does not want to penalize spades, typical ♠x ♥KQJxxx ♦Qxx ♣QJx. The same call after 1♠ is 2♠. Do not pull partner’s penalty double of their escape.

Example: ♠QJ54 ♥32 ♦KJ98 ♣K98 after 1♥ (X) XX (1♠) 2♥. The bid is pass.

Izdebski, School 7–9/2022.

## Do not always redouble on strength

Choose a bid that shows the suit or the fit when the redouble would only show points. Three cases:

- After 1♦ (X), a good suit and a diamond fit bids the suit, even above 9 HCP, and shows the diamonds later at the three-level. ♠53 ♥KQ983 ♦K1085 ♣Q10 bids 1♥. The same applies to 1♠ and 2♣ with a diamond fit. See [1♦ Interference](../openings/one-diamond-interference.md).
- Opposite a third-seat opening that may be light, pass with values and show the strength on the next round. A jump after an immediate redouble is read as preemptive. ♠AJ4 ♥32 ♦AJ876 ♣982 after P (P) 1♥ (X) passes. The forcing minor transfer is not used for that hand. It remains the method opposite a first- or second-seat opening, and a weak hand with a long minor may still transfer. See [1♥/1♠ Interference](../openings/one-heart-one-spade-interference.md).
- When our preempt is doubled, pass and double later if they escape. Do not redouble to show strength. Example: 3♦ (X) with ♠QJ76 ♥KJ32 ♦6 ♣A863. The bid is pass. The same pass applies when [3NT](../openings/three-level-openings.md) is doubled: redouble does not show stoppers. After [2♥/2♠](../openings/two-major-interference.md) is doubled, do not redouble. Any other call keeps the meaning it has when there is no double.

Izdebski, School 10–12/2019; 1–3/2022.

## Doubler’s partner after a redouble

When fourth hand redoubles, the doubler's partner is typically weak: the opponents and partner have shown the points. A simple suit bid promises no strength. It names the suit you prefer to play. Every suit jump is preemptive. 2NT shows at least 5-5 in the minors. Do not double their two-level escape without trump length, and normally pass partner’s preemptive jump.

The same bids apply when our reopening double is redoubled. Pass there is not a penalty pass of the redoubled contract.

The bids are tabulated under [Bidding after Opponent’s Redouble](take-out-doubles.md).

Example: after 1♥ (X) XX, bid 1♠ with ♠J964 ♥J1092 ♦J65 ♣63, and 2NT with ♠6 ♥104 ♦J10764 ♣109875. After 1♦ (X) XX, 2♠ is preemptive, typical ♠J10752 ♥98 ♦J86 ♣1092, and the doubler passes.

Izdebski, School 1–3/2015; 1–3/2022.

## Take-out double of a preempt

A double of a three- or four-level preempt is take-out. 4NT over their four of a major shows a 5-5 two-suiter, not Blackwood. Blackwood is unchanged once a suit is agreed.

Partner converts to penalty by passing in two cases: a worthless hand, or a reasonably strong balanced defensive hand with no safe road to game. With unbalanced offensive shape, bid.

Example: after (4♠) X (P), pass with the worthless ♠J73 ♥1064 ♦J8765 ♣104, and pass with the balanced ♠75 ♥K1064 ♦KQ95 ♣Q94. With ♠3 ♥KQ10874 ♦KJ95 ♣74 bid at the five-level. With ♠3 ♥QJ974 ♦QJ987 ♣42, 4NT is take-out in the red suits.

When they have already raised each other to at least nine trumps, the double is take-out with shortage in their suit. Bid your own suit. The sequence is (2♠) P (3♠) X.

Pass is the conversion. The positive bids in our [Lebensohl defence to 2♥/2♠](defence-specific-openings.md) stay as written when that defence applies.

Izdebski, School 4–6/2015; 7–9/2019.

## Sitting for partner’s double

When you have already shown a balanced hand and partner penalty-doubles, or reopens with a double, pass unless the hand has undisclosed extreme shape: a void together with 5-5 or with a six-card suit, or a suit of seven or more.

After 1♥ (1NT) X (P), or 1♠ (1NT) X (P), opener normally passes. The double is for penalty and shows about 9+ HCP. Pull at unfavorable vulnerability, with a side singleton, or with a very unbalanced hand. The penalty double after 1♣ or 1♦ (1NT) remains 8+ points.

Example: after 1♥ (1NT) X (P), pass with ♠J10 ♥AQ1074 ♦KQ8 ♣J92. Bid 2♦ with ♠J9 ♥K10654 ♦QJ876 ♣A.

After (1NT) X (P) against a weak 1NT, 8+ HCP and a balanced hand converts to penalty. A weak or unbalanced hand bids 2♣, pass or correct. The doubler passes with clubs and otherwise bids a suit. See [Defence to a Weak 1NT](defence-specific-openings.md). Our double of a strong 1NT remains Multi-Landy.

After a major opening, (1♥) P (1NT) P (P) X, and the same with 1♠, is a trap pass and therefore penalty. See [Take-Out Doubles](take-out-doubles.md).

A reopening pass is not forcing.

Izdebski, School 10–12/2014; 7–9/2015; 1–3/2018; 4–6/2018; 1–3/2015; 7–9/2019.

## Passes at the four- and five-level

A pass over their competitive bid at the four- or five-level is not forcing, at every vulnerability, especially when our own game bid was competitive. This includes (3♣/3♦) double (5♣/5♦). Once partner has doubled, for example (5♣) P (P) X, do not pull without a clear offensive reason. The gap between their game and our penalty can be small, and a double can make the play easier. The conclusion depends on the type of auction.

After a take-out double and their jump to four of a suit, pass is non-forcing. See [Take-Out Doubles](take-out-doubles.md) and [General Principles](general-principles.md).

Izdebski, School 1–3/2015; 7–9/2019.
