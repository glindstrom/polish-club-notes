# 1♥/1♠ Openings - Interference

??? note "Responses after 1♥ - (X)"
    ### Responses after 1♥ - (X)

    | Response | Description                        |
    | -------- | ---------------------------------- |
    | XX       | 10+; balanced hand                 |
    | 1♠       | 5-10; 5+♠, non-forcing             |
    | 1NT      | Transfer to ♣, forcing             |
    | 2♣       | Transfer to ♦, forcing             |
    | 2♦       | Transfer to ♥; ♥ fit, 0-6 or game force |
    | 2♥       | 7-10 HLDF; standard raise with ♥ fit    |
    | 2NT | invitational with fit |
    | 3♣       | 8-11; 5+♣, 3-card ♥ fit            |
    | 3♦       | 8-11; 5+♦, 3-card ♥ fit            |

    #### Note

    - After opponent’s double, 1NT and 2♣ are transfers showing 5+♣/♦, forcing, usable with weak or strong hands. 2♦ is a transfer to ♥ showing ♥ fit, either 0-6 or game force. 3♣/3♦ show own suit and 3-card ♥ fit, aiding game or part-score decisions. Other bids (e.g., 2♠, 2NT) are as without interference.

??? note "Meaning of Redouble After Interference"
    ### Meaning of Redouble After Interference

    The redouble shows a balanced hand with 10+ HCP. It invites opener to penalty-double their escape at the one- or two-level (Izdebski, School 7–9/2022; 10–12/2019). It does not ask to play the redoubled contract. With values in the opponent's suit, pass, to dissuade partner from bidding again.

    Opener’s free rebid in the opened suit is a minimum and not forcing. After 1♥ (X) XX (1♠), 2♥ is a good six-card suit, typical ♠x ♥KQJxxx ♦Qxx ♣QJx, and the redoubler passes. With ♠QJ54 ♥32 ♦KJ98 ♣K98 the bid is pass. The same applies to 2♠ after a 1♠ opening. Do not pull partner’s penalty double of their escape. See [Penalty Doubles, Redoubles and Passes](../competitive-bidding/penalty-doubles-and-passes.md).

    Opposite a third-seat opening that may be light, pass a minimum balanced hand and show the strength on the next round. A jump after an immediate redouble is read as preemptive (Izdebski, School 10–12/2019; 1–3/2022). A hand that fits a transfer still uses it: ♠AJ4 ♥32 ♦AJ876 ♣982 after P (P) 1♥ (X) bids 2♣.

??? note "Responses after 1♥/1♠ - (Overcall)"
    ### Responses after 1♥/1♠ - (Overcall)
    <a id="responses-after-1-1---overcall"></a>

    When an opponent overcalls our 1♥ or 1♠ opening bid, the following responses promise a fit in the opened major.

    | Bid             | Description                                  |
    | --------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
    | 2NT             | Game forcing with fit                        |
    | Opponent’s suit | Invitation to game with fit or "bad forcing" |
    | Jump to 4 in a new minor (e.g., 1♥–(2♠)–4♣) | Fit-showing: 4+ card support for opener’s major plus a good 5+ card suit in the bid minor; game values. Not a splinter. |

    - **Note**: For two-suited overcalls (e.g., 2♥ or 2♠ Michaels, or 2NT showing both minors after 1♥), see [Bidding After an Opponent’s Two-Suited Overcall](../after-intervention/bidding-after-two-suited-overcall.md).

    #### Note

    - The fit-showing jump to 4♣/4♦ is meant to help find a double fit (for slam or competitive decisions). It is not a splinter; with shortness, use other bids. (Source: Izdebski, Competitive Bidding School 7 – Responder’s Jumps, Continued.)
    - If responder is a passed hand, both bids (2NT and the opponent’s suit) invite game. 2NT is the stronger bid and promises a four-card fit.
    - If the opener has a broken honor sequence in the opponent’s suit, the best contract may be 3NT played from their hand. Therefore, responder with a hand otherwise suitable for 3NT bids the opponent’s suit (as "bad forcing"), while 2NT shows a distributional hand unsuitable for 3NT.
